The Solar System's planets are traditionally divided into terrestrial planets and giant planets. The four inner planets are rocky; the outer four are giant planets, with Jupiter and Saturn classified as gas giants and Uranus and Neptune as ice giants.

04 / Beyond our system

The galaxy
is bigger

There is an important distinction between the planets of our Solar System and the planets of the Milky Way.

The Milky Way contains billions of stars, and observations show that planets are common around stars beyond our Sun.

These worlds are called exoplanets. Thousands have been confirmed, but astronomers cannot currently identify and catalogue every planet in the entire galaxy.

Therefore, an honest planetary atlas should not invent a complete list of Milky Way planets. The eight worlds documented here are the planets of our own Solar System.

Accuracy note: “All planets of our galaxy” is not currently a finite catalogue available to science. This page deliberately avoids fabricated planets, fictional statistics and unsupported galaxy-wide totals.
